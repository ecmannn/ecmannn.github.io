I'm a Ph.D. Candidate in the Department of Political Science and the School of Global Policy and Strategy at UC San Diego. My research interests include international trade, emerging technology, and democratic backsliding, with a general focus on the political economy of globalization. 

My dissertation studies the political economy of export controls, the rules that stop firms from selling sensitive technologies abroad. When national security and commercial interests conflict, I ask how the state decides which products get controlled, who bears the costs, and what firms that lose sales receive in return. I answer these questions with a history of the American export control regime and three studies at different levels: the products on the control lists, the congressional districts that pay for them, and the firms that contest them through lobbying and public comment. 

I received my B.A. in Political Science / International Relations from UC San Diego, graduating *magna cum laude* and with High Honors, and I received my M.A. in Democracy and Governance from Georgetown University, where I graduated with High Honors.
