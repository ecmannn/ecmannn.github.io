I'm a fourth-year Ph.D. student in the Department of Political Science and the School of Global Policy and Strategy at UC San Diego. My research interests include international trade, emerging technology, and democratic backsliding, with a general focus on the political economy of globalization. 

My dissertation project examines the domestic politics of export controls. While export controls justified in the name of national security have grown exponentially over the past decade, the puzzle motivating my project is the variation beneath that rising mean: the restrictions we observe track the security threats meant to justify them surprisingly poorly. I argue that export controls are possible only where two conditions meet: a national-interest story must exist for the good, such that denying it would meaningfully degrade an adversary's capabilities, and the good must be excludable, such that a control denies the capability rather than merely diverting the sale to the next willing supplier. However, these conditions define where controls can happen, not who bears them. Because a control's costs concentrate on particular export-oriented firms and congressional districts while its security benefits diffuse across the nation, and because no economic constituency demands export controls, the politics that allocates them departs from the familiar politics of trade protection. My preliminary findings show that controls fall overwhelmingly on differentiated goods with high defense demand, and that legislators from exposed districts respond not by opposing controls but by seeking compensation. Where firms cannot contest a control in principle, I expect them to contest its technical thresholds instead. The central cleavage is thus not economic interests facing one another with the state as a neutral arena, but the state's own security interest set against an economy that would prefer to sell. 

I received my B.A. in Political Science / International Relations from UC San Diego, graduating *magna cum laude* and with High Honors, and I received my M.A. in Democracy and Governance from Georgetown University, where I graduated with High Honors.
